How it works

What the search does with your idea, where each fact on a card comes from, and what the tests measured. Every number on this page comes from a dated result file of a test.

The search, step by step

  1. Meaning. Google's embedding model turns your idea into a list of numbers that stands for its meaning, and the same was done once for the page of every project. The projects whose numbers are closest to yours come first. This finds the same idea in other words.
  2. Words. A word search (BM25) finds the projects that use the words of your idea. A rare word counts more than a common one such as "Hedera".
  3. The two lists together. The two lists are merged by the place of each project in each list (reciprocal rank fusion), and the first 10 are shown. No model chooses or orders them.
  4. The comparison. After the list is shown, a fast model (Gemini 3.8 Flash, at a low level of thinking) reads your idea and the texts of the 10 projects, and writes for each one what is the same and what differs. It only sees the projects that steps 1 to 3 found, it does not change their order, and it never judges a project.

The ways to search

The search page lets you choose how to search. Each way was measured on the cases of Test 2: pairs of past projects with the same idea, where the search must find one from the idea of the other, and ideas written by a person for a known project. A good way puts the known project in its first 10.

Where each fact comes from

  • The project's page, copied word for word by a program: the name, the short line, the texts, the links, the event and the prize. Each card links to its page.
  • The code and the Hedera ledger, read by a program: the repository and its dates, each Hedera ID named by the page or the code, and what the public mirror node says about it. Each ID links to HashScan, where anybody can see the same record.
  • A model (Gemini 3.1 Pro) writes seven fields of a card, such as what the project does and for whom. Each field carries a label and the sentences of the page it rests on, and a program checks that each sentence is really in the page. A field whose sentences are not found is not shown.

What this tool cannot do

  • It knows the projects whose page is still public, and only the winners of the events whose submissions are not public.
  • So far only the projects of the two test sets have a card written by a model; the others show their own short line, and have no category yet.
  • The order of a list says how close a project is to your idea. It never says that a project is good, bad, or a copy.
