# The ledger story, first measure: report of 23 October 2026

The question, the rules and the decision were written before any history was read: `guideline.md`, in two commits of 23 October 2026, the second one stricter. The result file is `evals/results/20261023T023136Z-ledger-signal.json`.

## What was read

- The step `history` read from the public mirror node every event of each own Hedera ID whose kind fits its page, on both networks when a page names none.
- The measure keeps each ID on its own network, where its status is the best, as the proof rule does when a page names no network (design 7.1, 7.2): 1,021 IDs of 254 projects. 13 of these histories reached the limit of 2,000 events and are cut. Counted once for each moment of consensus in a project, the IDs hold 64,078 events.

## The numbers

| Signal | Projects | Share | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1, active before: the project's own things were active before the window, and it has a proof found | 10 | 3.9% | 1.6% to 6.3% |
| S2, mostly before | 6 | 2.4% | 0.8% to 4.3% |
| S3, named but not done: the page names a service, and none of its IDs shows it | 77 | 30.3% | 24.8% to 35.8% |
| S4, done but not named: its IDs show a service at least 3 times, and the page does not name it | 70 | 27.6% | 22.1% to 33.1% |
| S5, came back (reported only) | 6 | 2.4% | 0.8% to 4.3% |
| **At least one of S1, S3 and S4** | **135** | **53.1%** | **46.9% to 59.1%** |

Each range is computed by project, as in the other tests.

## The decision

The guideline fixed the limit before the reading: the ledger story is worth building if at least 20% of the projects show at least one of S1, S3 and S4. The share is 53.1%, and even the low end of its range, 46.9%, is more than twice the limit. **The ledger story is worth building.**

## A check of the words, not the rule

The words that count as naming a service are strict on purpose. The same measure with looser words ("topic", "token", "NFT", "contract", "EVM", "schedule", "file") gives 190 of 254 projects (74.8%, range 69.3% to 79.9%): S3 rises to 170 projects, because a page that says "token" now names tokens, and S4 falls to 34. The decision does not hang on the strict words.

## What the signals say

- **The page and the ledger disagree most about services, not about dates.** S3 and S4 are common; S1, S2 and S5 are rare. Most projects made their own Hedera things inside the window of their event. So the part of the ledger story that compares what a page says with what its IDs did, service by service, is the part that matters most. The timeline matters for fewer projects.
- **S3 is a fact about what can be checked.** It says that none of the IDs that the page gives shows the service, not that the service was never used: a team may use a contract and give only its account. Contracts lead: of the 122 projects whose page names smart contracts or Solidity, 40 give no ID that shows a contract call.
- **S4 is often a strength that the page does not tell.** One page names only the Consensus Service, and its IDs also made 75 contract calls and 57 token events.
- Examples read by hand: a page that names the Consensus Service, whose IDs show only HBAR transfers (4 projects are like this); a page that names Hedera tokens, whose IDs show no token event (25 projects name tokens with no token event).

## The limits of this measure

- A project's IDs that its page never names are not seen.
- The words are a first choice. The check above shows that the decision holds either way.
- Activity before a hackathon can be allowed by its rules, and a page does not have to name every service. The signals are facts, not faults.
- The 13 cut histories may hold more events than were counted.
